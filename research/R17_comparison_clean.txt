    S2. THE CACHE QUESTION, TOOL BY TOOL (who can state NET dollars)


    Only SWEAP can currently state a net effect, and only at the token level,
    not yet the dollar level [ANCHOR].

      SWEAP: Has a real receipt from real counters on a 96.3% hit lane: 3
    breaks in one day, 207,566 tokens billed at full price = 2.02% of
    paid-lane input (upper bound), ceiling 333,120 if merged windows hide
    full-miss calls, baseline-corrected net ~202,003 (1.97%), attribution
    confirmed from store event records (B = compaction 95s prior; A and C =
    idle-expiry at 32.1h and 30min) [ANCHOR]. To convert token receipt to net
    dollars it needs the customer's actual per-lane price/rate [ASSUMPTION],
    and it must disclose the merged-window ceiling (207,566 vs 333,120)
    rather than report the floor as the answer [ANCHOR]. So: net tokens
    proven, net dollars one multiply away once the price is supplied.

      CAVEMAN: Cannot state a net dollar effect from our evidence, because
    33.2% / 0.46x are gross, vendor-reported, and not independently measured
    [THEORY]. Exposure: wire-plane re-encode can re-bill the prefix. On the
    measured 96.3% lane, if its compressed output mutates the encoded prefix,
    the next call can drop to 0% hit and bill full price (N2: one token
    mutation is enough) [INFERRED]. Its warm-cache-preservation claim
    [THEORY], if true, means its encoding stays stable and may protect the
    prefix, but it publishes no break receipt and cannot see breaks its own
    rewrites trigger or any store rot [INFERRED]. To prove net it must
    measure the same lane's hit rate with compression on vs off over a
    comparable window, count every re-encode and its effect on the billed
    prefix, and price the difference. Risk bearer: the customer, who pays
    every re-billed prefix at full price; caveman's 0.46x is gross, not net,
    unless it can show it did not cause prefix re-bills.

      JEV: Cannot state net today. ~100x cheaper/faster is a decision-task
    mechanism claim, not a cache receipt [THEORY]. Exposure: any elision
    changes the window and therefore the prefix, so the next call can miss
    and re-bill [INFERRED]. On the 96.3% lane, an elision between calls can
    drop the next call to 0% [INFERRED]. The pinned first message + newest N
    keeps a leading anchor, so a prefix cache keyed on the continuing head
    could still hit for the leading portion, but tail changes break the
    full-prefix match [INFERRED]. To prove net it must instrument the same
    lane's hit rate with compaction on vs off, count elisions, and measure
    re-billed tokens. Risk bearer: the customer again.

    Bottom line: only sweap has a measured net token number [ANCHOR];
    converting to dollars needs the lane price [ASSUMPTION]. Both caveman and
    jev are structurally exposed to prefix re-billing from their own
    mutations, on the exact axis N2 exposes (one-token mutation kills a 98.9%
    warm lane), and neither publishes a break receipt. They sell gross
    savings; none of the three sells the full net-dollar-received figure
    today.


    S3. WHERE THEY COMPETE, WHERE THEY COMPLEMENT (adopter's view)


    If a team already runs CAVEMAN: sweap adds the observation plane caveman
    lacks. Caveman tells gross tokens saved; sweap tells the real per-lane
    hit rate (96.3% vs 0% vs 1.0% vs 19.2%, N1), the actual break bill (2.02%
    receipt, N4), and whether each break was compaction or idle (N4
    attribution from store records). Because sweap reads the harness store,
    not a proxy, it is the only one that can independently check caveman's
    warm-cache-preservation claim against the customer's in-place counters.
    Complement: caveman reduces gross; sweap verifies net. Overlap fight:
    caveman's warm-cache claim lives exactly on the observation plane; if
    caveman's re-encode breaks a cache, sweap catches it and caveman cannot
    see it (it only ever sees the bytes it sent).

    If a team already runs JEV: sweap adds the same verification for the
    window: whether jev's elisions are re-billing prefixes and whether the
    survivor rows (kept verbatim + notes) match what jev elided, which is a
    data-integrity cross-check sweap's store census can run [INFERRED].
    Complement: jev shrinks the window; sweap verifies the bill and the
    store. Overlap: jev's main cache-break risk is exactly its window
    mutation, which is the observation plane's territory.

    Vice versa. If a team runs SWEAP (diagnosis only, read-only): caveman or
    jev adds the actual gross reduction sweap cannot achieve. On the 96.3%
    lane, caveman is mostly redundant for cost because the lane is already
    cached, so its compression helps mainly on the miss share; on the 0% free
    lanes there is no cache to preserve, so compression is the only lever
    [INFERRED, applying N1]. Jev helps where the store is large regardless of
    cache, because it shrinks the window itself.

    Honest overlap fights: (1) the observation plane collides with caveman's
    warm-cache claim and with jev's window-mutation risk; (2) the reduction
    plane, where sweap is absent, is where caveman and jev own the
    gross-savings story sweap cannot tell alone. Sweap's wedge is
    verification; caveman's and jev's wedge is change. They do not reduce the
    same thing, but they judge the same cache.


    S4. RED-TEAM (three strongest attacks on sweap, each with FALSIFIED IF)


    Attack 1: "Sweap's numbers are one host, one day, one lane. 96.3% and
    207,566 tokens are not a customer's recurring economics; you have a
    sample, not a proof." FALSIFIED IF: the same receipt derives from a
    replay of comparable real workloads on the same store instrumentation
    across multiple lanes and hosts over weeks, and if the harness store
    counters are shown to be provider-billed (the actual invoice), not
    estimated.

    Attack 2: "The observation plane is a temporary wedge. Caveman and jev
    will ship the same passive hit-rate dashboards and break logs natively,
    and the moment they do, sweap adds nothing a built-in dashboard already
    has." FALSIFIED IF: (a) sweap's store reading yields facts neither wire
    nor window planes can reach, notably the compaction-to-break attribution
    (B, 95s prior at N4) and store-rot visibility, which come from the
    harness's compaction-event records that neither proxy touches [ANCHOR].
    The attack only dies if (b) caveman/jev ship store-event capture and
    break attribution at comparable fidelity; if they do, the wedge genuinely
    narrows. This is the attack that assumes they ship the same
    observability, and it is the honest one to concede the most to.

    Attack 3: "Sweap's own instrumentation is unreliable, and it admitted it:
    N5 caught timestamp-not-a-unique-key, tick-read-as-idle, and an all-zero
    cache-write column. A tool that needed fixing to report correctly cannot
    be trusted to give a net dollar figure." FALSIFIED IF: the post-fix
    ledger (uid row keys, real per-lane idle, merged-window honesty markers,
    write-field trust stamp, automatic compaction-event join) reruns the same
    eleven-model blind audit with arithmetic exact 11/11 and no invented
    numbers or billing labels, and a fresh install reconstructs the full
    break history with zero integration [N4, ANCHOR]. The fact that N5 was
    caught and fixed is evidence of integrity, not unreliability [ANCHOR],
    but only the rerun audit defeats the residual-risk claim.


    S5. VERDICT


    Caveman is a wire-plane money-saver with vendor-stated gross numbers and
    no published receipt [THEORY]; it can claim it cut your tokens, but it
    cannot show its own net dollar effect after prefix re-billing, and it
    never sees store rot. Jev is a window-plane shrinker with a strong
    mechanism claim, a self-reported usefulness failure on its own research
    prototype, and no receipt, structurally exposed to the same prefix
    re-billing [THEORY]. Sweap is the only one of the three that today
    measures a net break cost from real counters, 2.02% of paid-lane input on
    a 96.3% hit lane with compaction-versus-idle attribution confirmed from
    store event records, and the only one whose claims are anchored rather
    than self-reported [ANCHOR]. The category's dirty secret, now measured,
    is that cache hit rate is a per-provider, per-lane contract, 96.3% on one
    paid lane and 0% on free lanes of the same platform, and a single token
    mutation drops a 98.9% warm lane to 0%, so blended marketing hit rates
    are meaningless [ANCHOR]. None of the three publishes a clean
    net-dollars-received figure for the whole bill, and converting sweap's
    token receipt to dollars still needs the actual lane price [ASSUMPTION].
    The single most important open measurement for the category is a
    standardized, provider-audited net-cache-break receipt: gross tokens
    saved minus every token the tool's own mutation re-bills, expressed in
    dollars on the customer's real lanes. [HYPOTHETICAL] whichever tool ships
    that receipt first and survives an external audit will own the trust
    layer of this category, because the other two can only claim gross
    savings. [HYPOTHETICAL] the window and wire mutation tools may eventually
    be judged less by how much they compress and more by how little they
    disturb the cache they sit in front of.
