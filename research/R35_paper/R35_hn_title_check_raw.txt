    REASONING (HN-specific)
    
    The list is a fast-scrolling, judgement-in-under-a-second surface.
    Readers never open your post to appreciate your title — they vote/skip in
    the same glance. What wins that glance is a concrete, self-measured
    number, because it converts instantly to "how did they know that?"
    curiosity without asking the reader to do any interpretive work.
    
    "Written Twice, Billed Once: The Defect Inside Agent Memory" fails that
    glance test in two ways. First, "Written Twice, Billed Once" is clever
    but opaque — the reader must translate it before it pays out, and
    translation in a scroll doesn't happen. Second, "The Defect Inside Agent
    Memory" is a book-jacket subtitle: it reads as stylized abstraction, and
    HN's anti-marketing filter is exactly tuned to bounce abstract,
    self-important phrasing. It doesn't signal substance; it signals a press
    release. It also happens to be the kind of title that drags the wrong
    crowd into the thread — generic "here's another AI agent hot take"
    arguers — before they've seen any receipts.
    
    "90.7% of Our AI Agent's Memory Is Dead Weight" is the opposite. It is a
    precise, non-round, honest number about your OWN system ("Our Agent"),
    which is the single most HN-native credibility move there is: measured,
    self-directed, no hype. The odd precision (90.7, not 90) reads as genuine
    measurement, not a rounded marketing figure. It creates a clean curiosity
    gap — "how do they know that?" — that the first comment must immediately
    reward.
    
    Which is exactly the handoff B sets up. The first comment is where clicks
    convert into repo visitors. So put the conversion engine there: open with
    the punchline ("Written twice, billed once") — the memorable line every
    reader will echo in the thread and actually quote back at you — then hand
    them the stat wall with the receipts. Because your launch comment's real
    job is not to be clever, it's to establish "we filed this, we blinded it,
    we double-derived it" fast enough that a skeptic scrolls to the repo
    instead of firing off a "source?" reply. The stat wall does that; the
    catchy title does not. In B, both surfaces are doing their highest-value
    job.
    
    Title restraint also favors B: 90.7% is exact and small, not an
    exclamation. It respects the norm that numbers on HN must be true — and
    they are your whole case.
    
    RISK A: The vague, stylized locked-title headline gets scrolled past in
    the list, so the post never earns the early upvote burst Show HN ranking
    depends on — your best material never reaches an audience because nobody
    clicked to see it.
    
    RISK B: In a post-HN thread, a few commenters will needle "number-led
    headline = linkbait" or snipe that the headline buries your actual thesis
    word ("defect"); the clever "Written Twice, Billed Once" line never
    appears in the main feed, so readers who never click the post miss it.
    Mitigation is built-in: your first-line comment surfaces the phrase
    immediately, and your receipts make the number defensible — but that
    sniping is the tax B pays.
