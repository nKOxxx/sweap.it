    Justification: the central "structural defect" is a bug in the authors'
    own product's schema on their own single store, and the paper's core
    dead-weight construct is internally contradictory (16.5M tokens are
    simultaneously "serve no future purpose" and priced precisely because a
    lapse re-transmits them), so the headline numbers are an n=1 self-report
    that no program committee can act on.
    
    Fatal flaw in one sentence: the manuscript bills 16,514,707 tokens as
    "dead" ("serve no future purpose") and then prices those same tokens
    being re-transmitted by a lapse, which is by its own trichotomy a future
    purpose, so the accounting at the heart of the paper is self-refuting.
    
    ════════════════════════════════════════════
    FINDINGS
    ════════════════════════════════════════════
    
    F1. NOVELTY / SELF-MEASUREMENT (the primary reject)
    Quote: "The Hermes schema stores model reasoning in a reasoning column
    and, on the same rows, a reasoning_content column." (Sec 2)
    The defect lives in the authors' own software (Hermes Agent, on their own
    reference host), and it is the whole novelty claim: "agent platforms
    persist model reasoning twice, byte-identical, under two column names"
    (Abstract; also Contribution 1). A top systems PC reads this as: you
    found a bug in a schema you wrote, on a store you operate. That is a bug
    report with a patch, or a diary entry ("we measured our own stores and
    found junk"), not a finding about agent memory. It does not generalize to
    "Agent Memory" — it is one vendor's two-column layout. Nothing
    independent, no co-authoring vendor, no second implementation.
    
    F2. EXTERNAL VALIDITY IS ZERO, not "limited"
    Quote: "16,514,707 of 18,210,064 stored transcript tokens - 90.69 percent
    by exact per-row count over all 37,813 tool rows ... serve no future
    purpose" (Abstract)
    Limitation 1 concedes: "one machine, one store, one month." Every
    headlined magnitude (90.69%, 26.0 MB, 431 rows/day, $8.09) is
    single-host, single-month, self-measured. The response ("the public
    receipt board invites outside hosts ... as receipts land," Sec 7) is an
    invitation, not evidence; at submission there are zero outside
    replications. Minimum a reviewer would demand: at least one external host
    executing the same census and regrowth measurement, and more than one
    calendar month. That minimum is unmet, so not one decimal in the abstract
    is today generalizable.
    
    F3. CONSTRUCT CONTRADICTION — the fatal one
    Quote (Sec 2, "dead"): "content that serves no future purpose in any
    state - never transmitted again in healthy operation, and re-priced at
    full input rate only in the lapse event"
    Quote (Sec 5.2): "A lapse that re-injected the entire dead subset
    (16,514,707 tokens) at full input price would cost $8.09 on GLM-4.6"
    The paper cannot have both. Its own trichotomy (live /
    cold-but-rereadable / dead) says the thing that distinguishes cold from
    dead is that cold is "transmitted again when the lapse happens." By that
    taxonomy, content that a lapse re-transmits is COLD, i.e. it serves a
    future purpose, i.e. it is not dead. But §5.2 prices exactly the "dead
    subset" being re-injected on a lapse and the $8.09 figure depends on that
    transmission. So the headline "90.69% ... serve no future purpose" flips
    to "these tokens are transmitted again, at a price" the moment you bill
    them. "Billed Once" and "serves no future purpose" are mutually
    exclusive; the accounting is circular.
    
    F4. ARITHMETIC MISLABELED IN SCALE HONESTY
    Quote: "A lapse that re-injected the entire dead subset (16,514,707
    tokens) at full input price would cost $8.09 on GLM-4.6"
    $8.09 = 16,514,707 × $0.49 (the GLM carrying-charge gap). Full input
    price for GLM-4.6 is $0.60, which would give $9.91. So the sentence says
    "at full input price" but uses the gap. Same for the companions: $2.23 =
    16.5M × 0.135 (gap, not $0.15 → $2.48), and $21.07 = 16.5M × 1.276 (gap,
    not $1.32 → $21.80). All three "full input price" figures are actually
    the charge-minus-cache gap, directly contradicting §2's "re-priced at
    full input rate." Either the lapse costs gap (then it is not full input
    rate, contradicting §2) or it costs full input (then the figures are ~20%
    too low). The label does not match the arithmetic.
    
    F5. CONSTRUCT OPERATIONALIZATION DOES NOT IMPLEMENT THE TAXONOMY
    Quote (Sec 4): "Content is dead if it is (i) a byte-identical duplicate
    ... (ii) attached to a session that has ended (resting exhaust), or (iii)
    bookkeeping that is never re-read (usage receipts)"
    The 90.69% is built on (ii): session-ended ⇒ dead. But the paper's own
    three states are live/cold/dead, and an ended session's content can be
    live (a rolled-up project summary a new session re-injects) or cold
    (re-injected on lapse). "Session-ended" is a proxy, not the construct
    "serves no future purpose." The census never measures a future read; it
    infers one from session end and then asserts "serve no future purpose."
    So the denominator is flattered: no evidence is given that any of the
    16.5M tokens was actually never read again, short of the lapse that §5.2
    itself assumes reads them.
    
    F6. REGROWTH "RATE" FROM A SUB-ONE-DAY WINDOW
    Quote (Sec 5.3): "the store regrew 384 tool rows (0.53 MB) in about 0.9
    active days: about 431 rows per day"
    The abstract promotes "about 431 rows per day" as a measured finding from
    a 0.9-day (less than one day) interval — a single extrapolation,
    presented without the caveat until §5.3's "measured rate on one host over
    one interval - not a law." A per-day rate from a sub-day window is one
    data point, not a rate; it belongs nowhere near the abstract.
    
    F7. THE PROTOCOL IS AN AGREEMENT TEST WITH NO POSITIVE CONTROL
    Quote (Sec 4): "It does not certify correctness against ground truth: a
    wrong constant planted in the brief would be reproduced by every blind
    lane" and "A proposed strengthening - deliberately poisoning a brief
    constant to test whether adjudication catches it - is noted for future
    work."
    So the protocol's only demonstrated capability is one hub arithmetic
    error (R36) caught out of 27 studies, and the poison/positive-control
    experiment is explicitly unimplemented "future work." Without a
    known-correct benchmark, "all lanes converged" is equally consistent with
    "all lanes wrong together." More importantly, the protocol operates on
    already-classified inputs; the largest subjective step — classifying rows
    as live vs dead (F3/F5) — is the hub's rule and is entirely outside the
    blind lanes. Blind lanes count what they are told to count; agreement
    among them says nothing about whether they were told the right thing to
    count. This is process theater relative to the rigor claims ("All derived
    figures passed a blind-worker protocol," Abstract).
    
    F8. THE HEADLINE CENSUS IS NOT AUDITABLE IN THE PAPER
    Quote (Contribution 2): "every row counted, n = 37,813 tool rows" and
    (Sec 4) "we count exactly, every row classified"
    The abstract and §5.1 state only the aggregate 90.69% / 16,514,707. The
    decomposition of those 18,210,064 tokens across live / cold / dead /
    usage-receipt / mirror classes — the very thing that would let a reader
    check the 90.69% construct — is not shown; it lives in R33, which a venue
    cannot act on. A hostile reader cannot tell whether receipts and mirror
    twins are inside or outside the 90.69%. The abstract says mirror is
    "additional to this figure," which implies receipts/mirror are NOT in the
    16.5M — undercounting true dead weight and making the headline a floor,
    yet the label '90.69% of stored transcript tokens' reads as the total
    dead fraction. The in-paper census table is missing.
    
    F9. TWO DIET/SWEEP MAGNITUDES, NEVER RECONCILED
    Quote (Sec 5.3): "the October 14 sweep removed 19.6 MB" — Quote (Sec
    5.4/Abstract): "removed ... 37.57 MB" (244.1 → 206.5 MB)
    The abstract's diet is 37.57 MB (R26), but the regrowth the abstract
    headlines (431 rows/day) was measured after a 19.6 MB sweep (R25). The
    reader cannot tell whether "regrowth after diet" refers to the paper's
    flagship 37.57 MB diet or an earlier, smaller 19.6 MB sweep, and the two
    numbers are never reconciled. Any number that appears twice and cannot be
    reconciled is a flag.
    
    F10. TITLE OVERCLAIMS A UNIVERSAL DEFECT
    Title: "Written Twice, Billed Once: The Defect Inside Agent Memory"
    "The Defect" (definite article) promises a general property of agent
    memory; the paper delivers one schema's two-column layout on one host.
    "Billed Once" is also unsupported by the paper's own economics: the
    duplicate is billed only in an unobserved, hypothetical lapse event (Sec
    5.2: "the ceiling for a wholesale re-read that we have not observed"); in
    healthy operation (§5.2: 95.87% cache hit) it bills nothing. The title
    overstates both the defect's scope and whether it bills at all. As a
    submission title it is provocative but false advertising.
    
    F11. "NO PRIOR WORK" IS CLAIMED, NOT ESTABLISHED
    Quote (Sec 2): "No prior work identifies the mirror-write defect,
    censuses what is at rest in these stores, prices the weight under vendor
    price lists, or demonstrates reversible removal. That gap is this paper."
    Because the object of study is the authors' own schema's internal column
    layout, "no prior work identifies the mirror-write defect" is close to
    vacuously true and unverifiable — nobody else has the file to check. A
    venue cannot evaluate a novelty claim scoped to the authors' private
    schema. The related-work section (Sec 3) surveys compressors and context
    managers, all of which operate on the wire/window, so the "unclaimed
    lane" framing is credible the other way out: it is unclaimed because it
    is not generalizable.
    
    F12. "100 PERCENT ACROSS MODELS" IS A SMALL-n ASSERTION
    Quote (Sec 2): "across every model on the host that writes reasoning, the
    twin rate was 100 percent (7,934 of 7,934 rows for glm-4.6, 22.12 MB
    duplicated)"
    Only one model's rate (glm-4.6) is given a numerator/denominator. Of the
    9,455 twin rows, glm-4.6 is 7,934; the other 1,521 rows across "every
    model" are asserted 100% with no per-model breakdown and no model count.
    If "every model" is two or three models on one Hermes host, the
    abstract's "100 percent twin rate across models" (and the title's
    universality) rests on a handful of model IDs on one schema. Report the
    model count and per-model twins, or drop the universal phrasing.

    WHAT WOULD FIX IT (per finding)
    ════════════════════════════════════════════
    
    F1: Reframe from "the defect inside agent memory" to "a
    duplicated-reasoning schema defect in Hermes, measured on one host," and
    demote it to a case study. Add a second, independent implementation of
    the same two-column pattern (or a vendor acknowledgment) before claiming
    it is a general agent-memory defect. Never present a bug in your own
    software as a discovery about the platform class.
    
    F2: Land at least one (ideally two) external-host receipt on the board
    before submission and cite it in the abstract; report a second month.
    Until then, every abstract magnitude must carry the single-host qualifier
    inline, not merely in Limitation 1.
    
    F3 (fatal): Reconcile the trichotomy with the census and the bill.
    Decide, per row, whether a lapse re-transmits it. If it does, it is cold
    and "serves no future purpose" is false — relabel the headline as
    "dormant/re-injectable weight" and separate the never-transmitted (truly
    dead) subset by measurement. You cannot both call 16.5M tokens dead and
    price their re-transmission. This is the single change that determines
    whether the paper's accounting is coherent.
    
    F4: Recompute Scale Honesty at the true full input price ($9.91 GLM) or
    relabel the sentences "$8.09 ... at the carrying-charge gap (P_input −
    P_cache)," and delete "at full input price." Make the label match the
    arithmetic everywhere.
    
    F5: Measure deadness, don't infer it: add a future-read trace (which rows
    are actually re-read in subsequent sessions) over the observation window,
    and report the live/cold/dead split as separate counts. The census must
    implement the stated construct, not approximate it by session end.
    
    F6: Report the regrowth measurement as what it is (384 rows over 0.9 days
    on one host), drop "about 431 rows per day" from the abstract, or extend
    the window to ≥ several full days.
    
    F7: Run the poisoning positive-control now (plant a wrong constant, show
    adjudication catches it) and report the hit rate, not narrative. State
    explicitly that the protocol validates arithmetic agreement only and that
    the dead/live classification is outside it. Do not advertise "hub-error
    detection" on the strength of a single erratum.
    
    F8: Put the full per-class census table in the paper (live / cold / dead
    / usage-receipt / mirror, with token counts summing to 18,210,064), so
    90.69% is decomposable and auditable without R33.
    
    F9: Reconcile 19.6 MB (R25) and 37.57 MB (R26) in one table with dates:
    which sweep each regrowth measurement follows, and whether the regrowth
    was measured after the flagship diet or an earlier partial one. Reconcile
    or remove one.
    
    F10: Retitle to something scoped and honest, e.g. "One Schema Stores
    Reasoning Twice: A Measured Duplication Defect in a Hermes Store" — drop
    "Billed Once," since §5.2 admits the billing event is unobserved.
    
    F11: Replace "No prior work / That gap is this paper" with a verifiable
    claim ("no prior work we can cite, on the public record, does X"), and
    scope the gap claim to what is on the public record, not your private
    schema.
    
    F12: Report the number of distinct models and per-model twin rows, or
    change "100 percent across every model" to "100 percent on the models
    observed (glm-4.6: 7,934/7,934)."
    
    Scorecard on the deliverable axes: Novelty fails (own-tool bug).
    Construct validity fails (dead/cold contradiction and mislabeled
    arithmetic, F3/F4/F5). External validity fails (n=1, zero replications).
    Protocol is agreement-theater with no positive control (F7). Writing
    overclaims in title and contribution framing (F10/F1). Internal
    consistency has a genuine self-contradiction (F3) and a number fed to a
    wrong price (F4).
    
    What the manuscript has in its favor, and I'd say so even as an enemy: it
    labels upper bounds, refuses to invent a Codex price ("we decline to
    invent one"), flags its own unreconstructed number (R32), and states its
    protocol's guarantee boundary instead of hiding it. Those are the habits
    of a careful engineer. But careful engineering of your own store,
    honestly reported, is a strong blog post and a good artifact release — it
    is not yet a top-venue systems paper, and the abstract, as written, asks
    the reader to believe three things (90.69% reliably dead, billed, and
    generalizable) the body's own definitions contradict.
