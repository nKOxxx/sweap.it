S1. WHY CLEANING TOOLS NORMALLY FAIL, AND THE ONES THAT DIDN'T

    The red-team attack is correct as stated: a cleaner marketed as a cleaner
    self-selects the already-sick. The historical record mostly agrees with
    them, but only for the ones marketed AS cleaning. The pattern that
    matters is that the tools that escaped did so by selling a different
    benefit than the cleaning.

    First, the failures (marketing-cleaner-as-cleaner):
    - Registry cleaners, disk defragmenters, junk-file utilities (mid-2000s
    era). These were marketed as "make your PC faster by removing junk." They
    ended up as bargain-bin bloatware, frequently prepackaged/bundled, low
    willingness to pay. [MED, industry-history knowledge; the class outcome
    is well documented, the specifics are general]. The trap: the pitch told
    the user "you are broken and cluttered," which only the self-diagnosed
    installed, and the felt benefit (PC speed) was small and hard to
    attribute to the tool.
    - CCleaner's ceiling as a utility. It DID grow large, but it grew on the
    free-scan mechanic (below), not on "I am a cleaner." It also demonstrates
    the vulnerability: acquired by Avast, then a supply-chain backdoor in
    2017. [HIGH, widely reported public incident]. Point: a utility has big
    distribution but thin trust and thin pricing power.

    Now the escapees, and the exact mechanic each used:
    1. The free scan that reveals a shocking number (CCleaner, CleanMyMac).
    You don't buy the cleaner, you run a free census and it tells you "14.3
    GB of junk" or "your Mac's avoidable waste is 27 GB." The reveal is the
    hook; the clean is the one-click payoff. [MED, widely documented product
    usage pattern]. This is the nearest precedent to our C1 scan. Its honest
    ceiling: the number must be big enough to shock, and users eventually
    discount it as fake or inflated ("the scan inflates to sell the clean" is
    a public perception). [MED, general user perception, not a measured fact].
    2. Fear + prevention repackaging (antivirus). AV escaped the sick-trap
    because it sells to the HEALTHY who are afraid. Nobody waits to be
    infected to install AV; the pitch is universal risk, not remediation of a
    present disease. [MED, industry norm]. This is the strongest inversion of
    the attack and the model for our C4 trust anchor: sell the identity of
    the protected, not the cure for the sick.
    3. The dashboard/status number (Datadog-class observability).
    Observability sells the felt benefit of SEEING, and the dashboard is a
    status object for the operator, not evidence of sickness. It is bought
    partly as competence/visibility, not as "my system is diseased." [MED,
    product-category knowledge]. Its ceiling: dashboards are vanity until
    they drive action, and on a desktop tool a "dashboard your friend can't
    see" has no social pull unless shared.
    4. Identity and the streak (Duolingo). The streak is a shareable artifact
    people post without being asked, and the identity "I am someone who shows
    up daily" is what converts a utility into a habit. Community became the
    marketing. [HIGH, widespread, publicly visible usage]. This is the model
    for our shareable mechanic in S5, but note Duolingo is additive (habit
    building), not subtractive (cleaning), which is why it is so shareable:
    it is flattering, not confessional.
    5. Trust as identity (1Password-class). The felt benefit is peace of mind
    and "I am the kind of person who takes my data seriously," bought
    pre-emptively by the healthy and the cautious. [MED, category knowledge].
    Like AV, it escapes by selling protection and identity rather than cure.

    The synthesis: cleaning tools fail when the frame is "you are sick, here
    is your medicine." They escape when the frame is (a) a shocking reveal
    the user wants to share, (b) protection against a universal threat, (c) a
    status object, or (d) an identity the user is proud of. In every escapee,
    the cleaning is the mechanism and a DIFFERENT benefit (money, fear,
    status, identity, privacy) is the message. Our job is to find which
    benefit the healthy already pay for, not to talk about vacuuming the
    context.

    S2. POSITIONING CANDIDATES FOR SWEAP

    C1 THE SCAN: free census as the hook
    - Pitch: "Run a free census. It tells you how much dead weight is sitting
    in your AI workspace, then clears it in one action you can undo."
    - WHO installs: The curious and the mildly annoyed as well as the
    drowning. A free scan is low-friction and curiosity is universal, which
    is exactly how CCleaner grew [MED, S1]. It does escape the niche for
    ENTTRY because a scan is not an admission of sickness.
    - Felt benefit: immediately, at the reveal. The number is the moment.
    - Honest weakness: the reveal re-labels the user as dirty ("57% of your
    workspace is dead weight" [HYPOTHETICAL, matches our [ANCHOR] 90.69% tool
    exhaust / 48% store file]). It hands the red team the same framing they
    attacked. Also the shocking-number mechanic is the most copyable of all
    five, and the number can come across as inflated, which erodes trust.
    - Needs hub expertise to demo? No. The product produces the census
    natively; the hub is not required.

    C2 THE RECEIPT: the waste detective
    - Pitch: "Your AI billed you for work you never asked for. Sweap shows
    you exactly what it was, and why it happened, every time."
    - WHO installs: anyone paying for AI, not just the drowning. The "system
    is robbing you" frame is universal to every paid user and is flattering
    to detect, not confessional. It inverts the attack: "I caught the machine
    overcharging," not "I was a mess."
    - Felt benefit: at the receipt, in money ("you lost 207,566 tokens today,
    here is why" [ANCHOR, tokens with attributed causes], converted to
    whatever the user's price is).
    - Honest weakness: our own measured waste is real but modest per event
    (2.02% of paid-lane input, ceiling 333,120, net ~202,003 [ANCHOR]).
    Framed in raw tokens it is a geeky number a non-expert can't feel; it
    must be converted to dollars, and we must NOT inflate it or we violate
    the net-effect honesty that is our differentiator. Also the receipt only
    lands on PAYERS, so it re-niches to people who spend money on AI, which
    is large but is not everyone.
    - Needs hub expertise to demo? No. Break attribution comes from store
    records (one compaction 95 seconds earlier, two idle [ANCHOR]). The
    receipt is the artifact.

    C3 THE REFEREE: the verification layer for the category
    - Pitch: "Run caveman and jev for savings. Run sweap for the truth about
    what actually reaches your context."
    - WHO installs: people already adopting caveman/jev. It rides the
    category's growth and positions sweap not as a niche cleaner but as the
    neutral truth-teller for the whole ecosystem.
    - Felt benefit: trust and certainty about your other tools, felt when a
    verdict contradicts a claim or confirms one.
    - Honest weakness: it is subservient and dependent on other tools'
    success, and "referee" and "verification layer" are mechanism words, not
    felt benefits. A non-expert does not wake up wanting a "verification
    layer." It also softens our own unique asset (net effect with attributed
    causes) into a generic "we check others."
    - Needs hub expertise to demo? Partially yes for the verdict to be
    credible; the causal attribution is our hard asset and the only one of
    the three tools that can state a net effect [ANCHOR].

    C4 THE TRUST ANCHOR: local-only by construction
    - Pitch: "The only context tool that physically cannot phone home. Every
    byte stays on your machine, on day one, no integration."
    - WHO installs: the cautious and privacy-conscious, who are mostly
    HEALTHY, which is the cleanest escape from the red team's trap (the
    AV/1Password model from S1). Privacy is bought by the healthy and the
    afraid, not the drowning.
    - Felt benefit: peace of mind, permanent, no event needed to feel it.
    - Honest weakness: trust is the slowest benefit to build and the hardest
    to prove. "Zero network calls in the product code" [ANCHOR] is a
    technical claim a non-expert cannot verify and must be taken on faith,
    and faith is a weak hook for a new tool. There is no felt MOMENT on
    installation; the payoff is the absence of something. It is also the most
    difficult to demonstrate without hub expertise, because demonstrating an
    absence requires the audience to trust the measurement.
    - Needs hub expertise to demo? Effectively yes. Proven impossibility is
    an expert claim.

    C5 THE LIGHT MACHINE: dead weight = slow and expensive, sweep = lighter
    - Pitch: "Your workspace holds dead weight that makes every session
    slower and every cache more expensive. Sweap removes it. Measured: 22.1
    MB and 14,955 provably-dead rows in one sweep [ANCHOR]."
    - WHO installs: speed and cost are near-universal wants, so it reaches
    beyond the drowning.
    - Felt benefit: a lighter, faster, cheaper workspace; felt most at the
    cleanup moment and in later session speed.
    - Honest weakness: the real felt speed change after a clean is often
    small for casual users, and the people who feel it most are the heaviest
    users, which is exactly the niche the red team said we were capped at.
    The 22.1 MB figure is honest but small-sounding to a non-expert who does
    not know what a good store size is.
    - Needs hub expertise to demo? No, the product measures it.

    S3. THE VERDICT

    Primary: C2 THE RECEIPT. Secondary: C4 THE TRUST ANCHOR.

    Why the receipt wins the selection-bias argument: it does not tell the
    user "you are a mess." It tells the user "a system you pay for is wasting
    your money and I can prove why." That framing applies to every paid AI
    user regardless of how healthy their workflow is, so it does not
    self-select the sick, and it is flattering to catch rather than
    confessional to admit. It is also the only one of the five built on our
    unique, defensible asset that no competitor can claim: the net effect
    with attributed causes (one break was a context compaction 95 seconds
    earlier, two were idle [ANCHOR]). Caveman and jev, per the founder's
    frame, deal in gross felt benefits; sweap is the only one of the three
    that can state a net effect instead of a gross claim [ANCHOR]. That is
    the differentiation, and the receipt is its natural voice.

    Why the trust anchor is the right secondary: it is the widest funnel
    because privacy and local-only are bought by the healthy and the
    cautious, the exact people the attack says we cannot reach. As the
    identity layer it lets the user self-select as "someone careful with
    their data" [ANCHOR: local-only by construction, zero network calls]
    rather than "someone drowning in context." It also reinforces the
    receipt's credibility: "we tell you this happened, and none of it left
    your machine" is a coherent pair.

    The combination reads: "Your AI wastes your money, we prove how, and it
    never leaves your machine." That is a felt benefit (money) plus an
    identity (privacy), and neither one requires the user to admit sickness.

    What it costs us, honestly:
    - Choosing receipt over scan gives up the widest, lowest-friction
    curiosity funnel and hands the free-census hook to a potential imitator.
    The scan reach is broad; the receipt reach is only paid users.
    - Choosing receipt over referee weakens the "complementary to caveman,
    never a competitor" framing [prior ruling]. We become the money-truth
    tool alongside them rather than riding their adoption as a neutral
    verifier.
    - Choosing trust as secondary rather than primary gives up selling pure
    privacy to the broadest healthy audience; privacy becomes our shield and
    moat rather than our headline, so the people who come only for privacy
    may not hear us.
    - The hardest cost is the honesty constraint itself: the receipt's drama
    depends on converting our modest measured waste (2.02% of paid-lane input
    [ANCHOR]) into a dollar figure that feels real, without inflating it. If
    the number is small in a given customer's terms, the receipt is quiet,
    and we must let it be quiet rather than pump it. We are betting that
    converting tokens to money plus a privacy identity carries a wider
    audience than the honest smallness of any single event might suggest.
    That is a real bet and we should say so.

    S4. THE ONE-SENTENCE CONTRAST

    "Caveman makes the request smaller. Jev makes the context faster. Sweap
    ___."

    Three options (a benefit a non-expert feels, not a mechanism):
    1. Sweap stops the waste before it is billed. (felt: money, honest
    because it is prevention, measured per event)
    2. Sweap proves where every token went. (felt: certainty/truth, but
    "token" is mechanism-adjacent for a non-expert)
    3. Sweap never sends your data anywhere. (felt: peace of mind, but it is
    the secondary not the primary)

    Pick option 1. "Stops the waste before it is billed" is the receipt in
    one line: a non-expert feels money and prevention, it is parallel in
    cadence to the other two, and it does not claim a cure for sickness.
    Option 2 leaks mechanism (tokens) and option 3 is our secondary. If
    option 1 must avoid the implied overclaim of "stops" (we stop the waste
    we find, we cannot stop what we have not run against), the honest
    fallback is "Sweap stops paying for AI work you never asked for," which
    keeps the money benefit and the prevention framing while being strictly
    true to the net-effect doctrine.

    S5. THE SHAREABLE MECHANIC + DISTRIBUTION

    The mechanic: the break receipt card, in effect the "AUDIT VERDICT." A
    single one-glance card that reads, in plain language: "Yesterday your AI
    billed you for work you never asked for. Cause: your lane sat idle, then
    compacted. Sweap caught it, locally, and none of it left your machine."
    It shows one measured number from the user's own machine [ANCHOR-derived:
    break attribution and token waste with causes], which is the thing that
    makes it shareable: it is real, it is the user's own data, and it is a
    small WIN ("I caught the machine"), not a confession ("I was a mess").
    That flips the red-team attack into a status object, the
    Dolingo-streak/identity model from S1 rather than the sick-patient model.

    What the card must NOT do, to stay inside the laws: it must not leak
    private transcript content, since local-only is our data point and our
    identity. Sharing must be a deliberate user action, redacted and
    aggregated by default, so that the act of sharing reinforces rather than
    contradicts the privacy position. The number shown shares only the
    verdict, never the bytes.

    Where it lives, product-led, no ads, no outreach:
    - CLI completion output prints the one-line verdict and offers to export
    a card, so the artifact is born inside the product the user already runs.
    - The free scan-only, no-signup census generates a shareable verdict
    card, which is the front door for the curious (the S1 free-scan mechanic)
    without requiring installation trust upfront.
    - Plugin directories and marketplaces (caveman and jev directories, per
    the complementary ruling) surface the verdict card as the README hero
    image, so the shareable artifact is also the listing that gets seen.
    - Word of mouth: people post "here is what my AI wasted today" the way
    they post streaks and screenshots, because it is flattering, one-glance,
    and carries a mildly provocative truth ("your AI overcharged you") rather
    than a self-diagnosis.

    Consistent with the laws: every number on the card is measured from the
    user's own machine and stamped as such, no fabricated stats, no fake
    urgency, no paid placement, no cold outreach. The weakness to name: a
    card that looks like an ad does not get shared, and a user whose measured
    waste is small gets a quiet card, and we must not gamify or inflate to
    make the quiet ones louder, because that would destroy the honesty that
    is our only moat.


    Bottom line for the founder, plain words: the red team is right that
    selling a cleaner as a cleaner caps you at the sickest niche. The escape
    is to sell a benefit the healthy already pay for. Your unique asset is
    not the cleaning, it is the proof: you are the only tool that can state a
    net effect with named causes [ANCHOR]. Put that proof in the format of a
    money receipt (primary) made credible by a local-only privacy identity
    (secondary), and make the receipt itself the thing people share. Give up
    the widest curiosity funnel and the ecosystem-referee role to get there.
    That is the honest trade.
