    1. TECHNICAL SETUP
    
    The product is a context-plane hygiene tool. The host plane is explicitly
    demoted to a small optional convenience surface and never leads the
    story, because the red team already settled that disk cleanup is
    commodity and fleets are ephemeral. Architecture is seven components with
    a defined order of execution.
    
    1a. Read-only snapshot census (foundation)
    Every measurement operates on a frozen read-only snapshot of a session
    store (SQLite for the host's own store, plus jsonl capture for live
    agents), never on the live store. This is the enforcement point of the
    self-sweep paradox law: the agent never scans its own write path. The
    census walks the snapshot with an external verifier that records a
    checksum manifest (per-session, per-file SHA-256) before any analysis. A
    snapshot is a directory of copied sqlite/jsonl artifacts plus its
    manifest; census reads only these. This component is v1 and non-optional;
    it is the substrate for everything else.
    
    1b. Measurement (stored bytes, then token economics)
    The census computes per-session and per-message stored byte counts, the
    distribution of tool-exhaust bytes (the 85.4% figure on this host), and
    re-derives the fixed per-call overhead (97.7KB on this host from system
    prompt + skills index + tool schemas). The plan separates two kinds of
    output. First, stored-bytes diagnostics, which are exact and cheap.
    Second, token-economics estimates, which convert stored bytes to
    prompt-side token share through a tokenizer (tiktoken or equivalent) on
    sampled real messages. The 85.4% number is a stored-bytes fact, and is
    reported as such; prompt-side truth requires tokenization plus real
    capture and is a v1.5 measurement, not a v1 claim [inference: because the
    source data makes clear stored bytes are not prompt bytes, quoting 85.4%
    as a token or cost fact would be unsupported]. Measurement precedes every
    destructive operation.
    
    1c. Fixed-overhead economics (the repitch)
    This component is the commercial heart. It computes, from the measured
    overhead bytes and the user's model pricing, the cost re-paid on every
    single API call before any conversation content arrives. On this host
    that is 97.7KB per call. The pitch becomes: every agent call carries a
    fixed toll; hygiene reduces the marginal content and, more importantly,
    quantifies how much of the paid context is dead weight. v1 exposes this
    as a per-call cost figure and a per-session breakdown. [inference: this
    is the differentiator the red team's fix #1 points to, so it belongs at
    the top of the UI, not buried.]
    
    1d. Distillation with provenance
    Before any reclamation, condensable content is distilled into
    representation with per-card provenance: session id, byte range, and
    checksum. This is the enforcement surface of the distill-then-sweep law
    and the Cognexia rule. Cognexia (the founder's memory product) is an
    optional, human-gated consumer of distilled cards, never an automatic
    sink. Each distilled card carries its provenance record and a
    retrieval-observability log (when a card is ever fetched, that fetch is
    recorded). Zero-fetch cards (never retrieved after N days) are themselves
    reclamation candidates. The agent does not self-certify that nothing
    vital was lost; instead the provenance and the before/after benchmark in
    1f provide external evidence. There is no agent-side assertion of safety,
    only an auditable trail. This is v1 for the provenance record and v1.5
    for the full Cognexia integration.
    
    1e. Gated reclaim (reversible first)
    Reclaim operates on the snapshot's conclusions but writes to the live
    store only through a quarantine step: matched content moves to a
    quarantine bucket with a duration, and only after the duration and a
    human or history-scoped confirmation does it become eligible for delete.
    This is the enforcement point of default-to-reversible and
    human-gate-for-judgment-calls. The hard-deny set comes from the GC
    mark-sweep law adapted verbatim: never sweep reachable state. Anything
    referenced by a live session, a pointer, or a memory that is still
    load-bearing is in the hard-deny set and excluded. The generational
    hypothesis (most objects die young, so does most context) informs the
    age-based eligibility tiers but is not a license to delete; it only
    shapes the candidate list. DB surgery is never reimplemented; mutation
    goes through the store's own APIs or a thin, tested adapter, which is the
    enforcement point of never-reimplement-DB-surgery.
    
    1f. Before/after benchmark harness
    The long pole and the honesty floor. A held-out task set is frozen up
    front. Each measured session runs against the harness before and after a
    reclaim to produce a paired score. This is the enforcement point of the
    ordered fix #2 (frozen-snapshot before/after with a held-out set before
    more sweep features) and of the founder's measurement-before-destruction
    law applied to the product's own behavior. The harness records tokens
    saved and any task-quality delta. Nothing ships to promotion without a
    paired result on the held-out set. This is v1 and is the gate that blocks
    feature creep.
    
    1g. Host-plane convenience (optional, later)
    A minimal surface: du-based size report, stray screenshot and log sweep
    into quarantine. This is a thin wrapper over find, du, and cron, which is
    exactly why the red team says it is commodity. It ships as a single
    toggle, clearly secondary, and is v2 at the earliest.
    
    Data flow, end to end: live store to frozen snapshot and manifest,
    snapshot to stored-bytes and token-economics measurement, measurement
    output to candidate lists, candidates filtered through the hard-deny set,
    survivors distilled with provenance, distilled cards optionally surfaced
    to Cognexia (human-gated), quarantine applied to the live store, then the
    held-out harness run before and after to certify no regression. The
    snapshot, measurement, and benchmark components are read-only against the
    source of truth; only the quarantine write touches the live store.

    3. GO/NO-GO
    
    Arguments for go.
    The market positioning is defensible because it is narrow. The verified
    landscape has syntax cousins (MemGPT/Letta pages memory but does not
    measure fixed overhead, has no gated reclaim, no host plane) and a
    monitor class that measures but never cleans (splitrail, blitzclean, the
    usage-analyzer family), plus a graveyard of single-CLI hacks with no two
    planes, no reversible reclaim, no distill-before-sweep. Nobody in the
    271-record harvest offers the combination of read-only snapshot census,
    measured fixed-overhead economics, and reversible, benchmark-verified
    reclaim. That gap is real and now demonstrated, not asserted [inference:
    the harvest supports the gap claim strongly]. The fixed overhead is a
    concrete, re-paid-every-call cost that is easy for an engineering buyer
    to feel immediately on their own invoices, which gives the pitch a
    natural edge over the nebulous "clean your AI." The safety design
    (hard-deny set, quarantine, held-out harness, provenance) directly
    answers the red team's three risks, so the objections most likely to kill
    a hygiene tool have prescribed engineering answers already. The paper is
    a parallel asset: several of the cited academic works argue case by case
    for context as a priced lifecycle, and even if the product stalls, the
    measured census plus the fixed-overhead economics is a publishable
    empirical contribution.
    
    Arguments for no-go.
    The red team's F2 verdict is damning if read at face value: the CCleaner
    frame failed because fleets are multi-host and ephemeral and disk is
    disposable, and the scarce resource is priced context tokens, not disk.
    The product pivots away from disk cleanup toward the token plane, which
    is the correct response, but it inherits a harder question the facts do
    not resolve: who pays. Agent-tool developers are a thin and
    price-sensitive market, teams may not own a single-store census at all
    (they spread across hosts), and the "nobody" outcome is not excluded by
    the 271 harvest, which shows close cousins but no demonstrably paying
    buyers. The differentiator is real but narrow: it is one measured insight
    (fixed re-paid overhead) plus a hygiene loop that the market has
    repeatedly failed to monetize (the graveyard). Effort concentrates
    exactly where the risk concentrates, in the benchmark phase, which is
    also honest and slow. There is also a trust asymmetry that no amount of
    good design fully removes: deletion tooling asks users to hand over the
    thing they fear losing, and the self-sweep paradox means the tool's own
    assurance can never be fully self-issued, so marketing against that fear
    costs more than the engineering.
    
    Verdict: GO, with strict gating on the benchmark phase as the committed
    kill-or-continue point.
    The reasoning: the only thing the facts truly rule out is building a
    host-plane CCleaner, and the plan does not. The context-plane thesis
    survives the red team's own logic, because red team fix #1 explicitly
    repivots to fixed overhead, which is measurable and priced. But the
    go-ahead is conditional and priced on evidence, not faith. The market
    question (who pays) is not answered by any record in the harvest, so it
    must be answered by a real buyer signal, and the cheapest honest buyer
    signal the product can produce is the before/after token-savings and cost
    number on a real store, which is exactly what the benchmark phase
    produces. That is why the verdict is a conditional go whose main exit
    hinges on the benchmark and on a live willingness-to-pay check, not on
    more features.
    
    Kill criteria (any one observed result stops the project).
    K1: The held-out benchmark harness shows no meaningful, reproducible
    token saving after reclaim without a task-quality regression, or shows a
    regression on the held-out set that provenance cannot explain. If reclaim
    cannot demonstrably help the thing it claims to help, the product has no
    reason to exist.
    K2: After a live-opt-in experiment, no paying or pre-paying buyer (single
    engineering lead, small team, or explicit LOI-equivalent interest)
    emerges from a cold outreach of, say, 10-20 agent-tool developers; verify
    with real replies, not inbound signups. If nobody pays, it does not
    matter how good the harness is.
    K3: A safety failure class is observed in the wild, specifically a
    reclaim that moves or deletes load-bearing state from the hard-deny set,
    or a provenance record that checksums as modified after the fact. Loss of
    trust in a deletion tool is unrecoverable and the founder's own laws
    forbid self-certification as a defense.
    K4: The fixed-overhead number, once measured with real tokenization and
    capture, turns out to be a minor share of a typical paid bill in the
    developer segment. The whole repitch stands on that number being
    material; if it is small, the pitch collapses back into the doomed
    CCleaner frame.
    K5: External verification of the self-sweep protocol fails, meaning the
    census cannot be reproduced by a read-only observer. If the tool cannot
    demonstrate it is not grading its own homework, the trust asymmetry
    argument above wins.
    
    Any one of these is sufficient. The most likely, by effort concentration,
    is K1 or K2, and both land at or just past the benchmark phase, which is
    the planned decision gate. That is an honest structure: heavy spend
    happens only up to the gate, and the gate itself produces the evidence

    blitzclean as monitor-only, but a product claim that they now clean, or

    roadmap. Two hours, no citation risk.

    no paying buyer. Cheap check: run the founder's own outreach of 10-20
    agent-tool developers with a one-paragraph fixed-overhead pitch and count
    replies with a genuine pain point or budget. This is the only way to
    convert who-pays from an open question into data, and it costs nothing
