    SECTION 1: FIT ANALYSIS
    
    I evaluated Cognexia against each accepted red-team condition from wave
    R2. The result is uncomfortable: the conditions that look like a product
    gap are not bugs in Cognexia, they are the entire product domain of
    sweap.it, and Cognexia was never built for that domain. It is a retention
    product, not a hygiene product. That is the root mismatch.
    
    Condition-by-condition:
    
    1. Preservation optional and human-gated. Cognexia is a general memory
    writer. Its 37 HTTP endpoints include write paths, so an agent can insert
    content programmatically without human approval. Nothing in the described
    schema or API blocks that. A human-before-write gate is not a property of
    Cognexia; it is a workflow wrapper sweap.it would have to impose from
    outside. Satisfiable only by extension, and even then it is enforced at
    the sweap.it boundary, not inside Cognexia, so it is a trust-on-honor
    walk behind. Fails natively.
    
    2. Provenance on every card (source session id, exact byte range,
    checksum). Not listed anywhere in the Cognexia data model. SQLite plus
    Markdown, importance scores, project isolation: none of these is a
    provenance field. You could stuff session id, byte range, and checksum
    into Markdown front matter or a parallel SQLite column by convention, but
    that convention is not enforced by the schema, so a card can silently
    lose its receipt. In a product whose entire guarantee is "nothing vital
    lost, and if it is, you can prove what and where," a convention-based
    provenance is a liability, not a feature. Needs substantial extension
    work, and the current architecture does not make it natural.
    
    3. Retrieval observable. Retrieval observability (log every fetch, expose
    fetch counts) is not a described Cognexia capability. The endpoint
    surface suggests you could add a logging shim, but again, sweap.it would
    be bolting it on. Not native.
    
    4. Cards never fetched must get swept. This is the direct failure.
    Cognexia's whole purpose is to keep memories. Nothing in it sweeps,
    deletes, or reclaims unreferenced cards. The instruction "reclaim what is
    never used" is opposite to its design intent. This condition is
    effectively impossible in the current architecture; you cannot rely on a
    retention product to be the actor that destroys stale content safely. It
    would have to be a sweap.it process reaching into Cognexia's store and
    deleting rows, which is exactly the fragile situation the product laws
    warn against (never reimplement DB surgery, measurement before
    destruction, default to reversible). Fails hard.
    
    5. No agent self-certification, external gate mandatory. Not native.
    Cognexia stores what it is told to store; it has no concept of an
    external human acceptance gate on destruction. Extension work only.
    
    6. Fatal variant prohibited by construction (card is a view, source stays
    quarantined until a human accepts). The quarantine lives in the source
    store (the 227MB sqlite plus host-plane), not in Cognexia. Cognexia holds
    only the view side. Keeping the two consistent under the rule "source
    must not be destroyed until the card is accepted, and the card must never
    be the only copy before acceptance" requires a bidirectional,
    transactional relationship between the source sqlite and Cognexia's
    store. That is a two-store consistency problem the current architecture
    does not solve and does not appear designed for. Impossible as specified,
    extension only at best, and fragile.
    
    What Cognexia does bring, honestly: importance scores are a usable
    retention prior, and interestingly they invert into a sweep-candidate
    selector (low importance is a first-pass candidate for destruction, which
    actually supports the reclaim loop rather than fighting it). That is a
    real, non-trivial contribution. And SQLite plus Markdown is a duality
    that maps reasonably to "queried index" (SQLite) plus "human-readable
    card" (Markdown). [inference] Both are useful signals, but neither is a
    red-team condition; they are conveniences layered on top of a backbone
    sweap.it still has to build itself.
    
    Project isolation versus fleet reality is also a mismatch. [inference]
    Cognexia's project model is single-machine, single-user mental model: one
    person, projects as folders. Sweap.it operates on a fleet: 143 sessions

    isolation. You would be stretching a personal-memory abstraction to serve

    SECTION 2: DEPENDENCY RISK
    
    Founder-owned, single-maintainer. Sweap.it is a hygiene tool for LLM
    agent stacks whose stated laws include "measurement before destruction"
    and "default to reversible." Coupling its irreversible-safety backbone to
    a one-maintainer product is architecturally ironic: a tool that exists to
    decouple cleanly gets coupled at its most safety-critical seam.
    
    MIT license is necessary but far from sufficient. It protects against
    license lockout and commercialization: you could fork and continue even
    if Cognexia disappears. But MIT does nothing about maintenance failure,
    pivot, or API drift. If Cognexia stagnates, sweap.it's preservation loop
    freezes. If it pivots, the 37-endpoint surface changes and the adapter
    breaks. If it breaks outright, sweap.it's guarantee is compromised at the
    exact moment it matters.
    
    Exit cost if integration must be reverted: medium and asymmetric. The
    danger is not the initial adapter code, it is the two-store consistency.
    Cards would accumulate inside Cognexia's sqlite and markdown, keyed by
    sweap.it provenance that ultimately lives outside. Reverting means
    migrating or abandoning every card written into a store with a foreign
    schema and unknown integrity. Because grave failure of this layer by
    definition happens when data is already stressed (post-reclaim,
    post-quarantine), the worst-case exit is expensive precisely when you are
    least able to afford it. [inference]
    
    There is also a second-store maintenance burden unrelated to Cognexia's
    health: sweap.it would need to keep the source store and Cognexia
    consistent on every operation. That is real recurring cost and a new
    class of bugs the product did not otherwise have. [inference]
    
    SECTION 3: ALTERNATIVES
    
    Option (a), minimal native card store embedded in sweap.it, schema
    exactly: session id, byte range, checksum, card text, fetch counter. Note
    this is not reimplementing DB surgery; it is a five column view table
    over session data. It directly materializes the red-team backbone:
    provenance (session id plus byte range plus checksum are the hardware of
    the receipt), observability (the fetch counter is not a decoration, it is
    the exact hook that makes "cards never fetched get swept" implementable
    and checkable), and the human accept gate is a status column away. Effort
    low, risk low, no dependency, no two-store consistency. Its weakness is
    zero retention-prior intelligence out of the box; you lose the
    importance-score prior unless you add a low-value column yourself
    (trivial).
    
    Option (b), other memory systems from the harvest category, generic
    local-first memory. The 271-record harvest already found no system
    combining hygiene, gated reversible reclaim, and distill-before-sweep
    with provenance. So the generic alternative inherits someone else's
    schema and mental model (importance scores, graphs, RAG) that matches
    none of the red-team conditions, adds unknown maintenance and license
    risk, and typically is pull-retention oriented, the opposite of what you
    need. Scores poorly on provenance, observability, and risk; effort is the
    highest of the three. Reject unless a specific listed system has native
    byte-range provenance, which the harvest did not surface.
    
    Option (c), Cognexia, per sections 1 and 2.

                            provenance   observability   effort  risk
    Native card store        5            5               1      1
    Cognexia                 2            2               3      4
    Generic local-first      1            1               5      5
    
    By every axis the native store wins. Cognexia's only advantages are the
    importance-score prior and the convenience of an existing product,
    neither of which is a conditional requirement.
    
    SECTION 4: VERDICT
    
    SKIP-BUILD-NATIVE.
    
    The honest answer is that Cognexia does not satisfy the accepted red-team
    conditions natively; every condition is extension work or impossible in
    its architecture, and the two that matter most (cards-never-fetched must
    be swept, and source-quarantine-till-human-accept) run counter to
    Cognexia's design intent as a retention product. Coupling the safety
    backbone to a single-maintainer dependency adds two-store consistency
    risk precisely where sweap.it's guarantee must be strongest. The
    five-column native card store implements the proven backbone at low cost
    with no dependency and no consistency seam, and it makes the fetch
    counter and the human gate first-class schema, not conventions.
    
    Let Cognexia remain what the R3 architecture already scoped it as: an
    OPTIONAL downstream consumer. The native store is the backbone; Cognexia
    may subscribe to cards for its Markdown-view reading and importance-score
    conveniences, human-gated, and fully removable without touching the
    guarantee. That keeps its genuine value (readable cards, retention prior
    on top) while moving it out of the safety-critical path. [inference] This
    reconciles the R3 "optional Cognexia consumer" note with this verdict: it
    was always optional, and it should stay optional as a consumer, never as
    the backbone.
    
    Decision conditions that would flip this verdict:
    
    Flip to INTEGRATE-WITH-EXTENSIONS if, in Cognexia core (not sweap.it
    wrappers), all three land: (1) first-class provenance columns for session

    redundant. That is a fleet-reality change, not a feature change.
    
    Stay SKIP if the founder's default is low risk, no new dependencies, and

    The current evidence does not justify INTEGRATE, and
    INTEGRATE-WITH-EXTENSIONS only if Cognexia adopts the three core changes,

    Plainly: the founder asked for pushback over loyalty. The pushback is
